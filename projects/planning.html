PROJECT STORY

Before the first brick.

01

The problem

The feasibility study examines gaps in public transport, reliance on private vehicles, transfer congestion, and pedestrian access along the Boavista corridor.

02

My contribution

Six months of pre-construction work covered architectural, structural, and MEP coordination using BIM and Navisworks; Primavera P6 schedules supported 4D sequencing. Model-based takeoffs and Bluebeam validation informed cost planning.

03

Estimation

The documented workflow moves from a conceptual estimate using gross area and RSMeans to drawing-based quantities, a design-development baseline, and a CSI MasterFormat breakdown. The study establishes a total estimated project cost of $60,082,987.

04

Scheduling & risk

The study programme contains approximately 300 activities, about 100 identified as critical, and a 530-day duration. Its risk discussion covers imported curtain-wall procurement and weather-sensitive excavation.

05

Design & environmental response

The proposal connects a high-capacity transit hub with its urban context. The feasibility study identifies walkability, cycling, public transport access, and green corridors as intended benefits; these are design intentions rather than measured outcomes.

06

Deliverables & outcome

A feasibility study, coordinated model views, plans and sections, a detailed estimate, a Primavera P6 programme, and critical-path documentation make the relationship between scope, cost, and time reviewable. The study covers a 260,000 sq. ft. gross built-up area and a design-build delivery strategy.
